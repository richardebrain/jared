You are in a preschool classroom with 15 children aged 4-5 years. The day begins with circle time where the children share their weekend activities. You notice that Tommy, usually enthusiastic, is unusually quiet. As the session progresses, you observe Tommy becoming disengaged, fidgeting, and not participating.

**Decision Point 1:** How do you address Tommy's behavior?

**Option A:** Ignore Tommy's behavior and continue with the session, thinking he might just be having an off day.

**Option B:** Gently ask Tommy if he wants to share anything or if something is bothering him.

**Option C:** Publicly point out Tommy's lack of participation to get him engaged with peer support.

---

**Consequences:**

- **Option A:** By ignoring Tommy, he remains disengaged, possibly reinforcing his feelings of discomfort or anxiety. The opportunity to understand and support him is missed, potentially affecting his confidence and participation in future activities.

- **Option B:** Tommy looks up and hesitates, but then starts sharing that he had a fight with his brother over the weekend. This opens a supportive dialogue, and you acknowledge his feelings, offering a reassuring presence. Tommy becomes more involved as the day goes on, feeling that his emotions are respected and validated.

- **Option C:** Tommy becomes embarrassed and further withdrawn, feeling singled out. This approach may lead to a decrease in his willingness to participate in future class activities due to fear of public criticism.

---

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Recognizing and acknowledging individual student needs can foster a supportive and inclusive classroom environment.
- Positive behavior interventions, such as private, gentle inquiries, can encourage student engagement and emotional expression.
- Publicly addressing a student's behavior in a critical manner may lead to negative emotional and social consequences, discouraging future participation.

This scenario highlights the importance of tailored positive behavior strategies in building a nurturing and responsive classroom culture.
