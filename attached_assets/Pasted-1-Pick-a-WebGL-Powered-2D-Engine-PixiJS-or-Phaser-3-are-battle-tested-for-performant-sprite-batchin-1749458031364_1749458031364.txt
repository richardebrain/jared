1. Pick a WebGL-Powered 2D Engine
PixiJS or Phaser 3 are battle-tested for performant sprite batching, particle systems, shaders and texture atlases.

Wrap them in React with react-pixi or a custom hook so your game canvas lives alongside your UI components.

2. Sprite Atlases & Texture Packing
Use a tool like TexturePacker or Shoebox to pack all your character, obstacle and UI art into one or two PNG atlases.

Load them once, then create PIXI.Sprite (or Phaser.GameObjects.Sprite) instances for each obstacle/teacher avatar—no per-frame image loads.

3. Offscreen & Parallax Layers
Render static background layers (lanes, sidewalks, playground buildings) to offscreen framebuffers or RenderTextures, then composite them each frame.

Create two or three of these at different scroll speeds for a parallax effect.

4. Tweened, Grid-Snapped Movement
Use a tweening library like GSAP or the engine’s built-in tweens to move your teacher sprite from lane to lane over 0.1 s with ease-in/out.

On each arrow-key (or tap), fire a single tween and ignore new input until it completes—guarantees crisp, non-overlapping moves.

5. Particle & Feedback Systems
Implement a pooled ParticleContainer (PixiJS) or ParticleEmitter (Phaser) for confetti bursts and near-miss sparks.

Cap particles at ~30 per burst and reuse them to avoid GC spikes.

6. Shader Effects & Bloom
Add a simple glow filter (e.g. PIXI.filters.GlowFilter) around power-ups when they spawn.

Use a full-screen color tint flash on collision (graphics.beginFill(0xFF0000, 0.2) + fade) for instant feedback.

7. High-Resolution Canvas & Downscaling
Render your WebGL canvas at 2× the logical resolution (e.g. 1200×1600) with antialias: true, then CSS-scale it down to 600×800—gives crisp, sub-pixel edges.

8. Fixed Timestep & requestAnimationFrame
Drive your game loop with a fixed-timestep update inside requestAnimationFrame, interpolating render frames to maintain a rock-solid 60 FPS.

9. UI Overlays in React
Keep your heads-up display (score, quiz dialogs, buttons) as React components overlayed on the Canvas.

Use React state to push data into Pixi/Phaser (e.g. new quiz prompt → pause the renderer and show your <QuizModal />).

10. Asset & Performance Safeguards
Preload and .baseTexture.preload() all atlases before the first run.

Monitor your render stats (e.g. Pixi’s app.ticker.FPS) and dynamically throttle spawn rates or particle counts if you ever drop below ~55 FPS.

In short: • Use a WebGL-first 2D engine (PixiJS/Phaser) • Pack and batch your art in atlases • Pre-render static layers • Tween grid-snapped moves • Pool your particles • Add simple shaders/filters • Render high-res + downscale • Drive your loop with requestAnimationFrame + fixed timestep • Overlay UI in React.

That roadmap will give you the modern, polished look and buttery-smooth feel of a top-tier endless runner—perfect for keeping anyone hooked while they learn.
