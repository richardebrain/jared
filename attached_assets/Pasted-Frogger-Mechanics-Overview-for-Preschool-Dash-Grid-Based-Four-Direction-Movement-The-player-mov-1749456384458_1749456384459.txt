Frogger Mechanics Overview for “Preschool Dash”

Grid‐Based, Four-Direction Movement

The player moves one fixed “step” at a time with each arrow key press (Up, Down, Left, Right), snapping to a simple row-and-column grid.

No analog or sliding—each tap moves the avatar exactly one lane up/down or one column left/right.

Fixed Lanes of Horizontal Traffic

The playfield is divided into a series of parallel horizontal lanes (typically five).

Each lane has obstacles that continuously travel left or right at a constant speed.

When an obstacle leaves one side of the screen, it immediately reappears on the opposite side—creating an endless stream.

Collision = Reset / Penalty

If the player’s sprite overlaps an obstacle’s rectangle, it triggers a “hit.”

In classic Frogger, that means losing a life and restarting at the bottom. In our version, it pauses for a quiz question, deducts a life if wrong, and respawns the player at the last checkpoint.

Top-of-Screen Goal

The goal (in Frogger it’s a safe lily pad, here it’s the runaway child) sits in the topmost lane.

Reaching that top lane cell exactly with no collision triggers a “level complete.”

Increasing Difficulty by Level

Each successive level speeds up all lane traffic and/or adds more obstacles per lane.

You can also introduce new hazard types (e.g. faster vehicles, staggered spawn timings) to keep the challenge growing.

Checkpoints & Safe Zones

To avoid frustration, implement a mid-level “safe zone” (e.g. after passing the third lane). Collisions only send you back to that checkpoint, not all the way to the bottom.

Power-Ups & Bonuses

Scatter collectibles in certain lanes that confer temporary shields or speed boosts.

These pop in and out of a lane like obstacles but are colored differently and tagged with text.

Consistent Spawn Timers

Rather than dumping all obstacles at once, each lane has its own spawn timer so hazards appear at regular, predictable (yet varied) intervals—players learn to time their moves.

Visual Clarity

Obstacles use high-contrast colors and bold outlines so players immediately know what to dodge.

The grid and lanes are clearly marked so movement and positioning feel intuitive.

Flow & Feedback

Lock the frame rate (60 FPS) and use easing so each grid move feels weighty but precise.

Add small animations (screen shake on near miss, particle bursts on success) and audio cues (“boop” on collect, “buzz” on hit) to reinforce actions.
