Switch to a Modern Rendering Engine

Use the Python Arcade library (built on OpenGL) instead of raw Pygame for GPU-accelerated sprites, smoothing, and scene management.

High-Resolution Sprite Sheets

Provide or link to 2–4x HD PNG sprite atlases for the teacher, child, obstacles, power-ups, and UI.

Load them with arcade.load_spritesheet and use arcade.SpriteList to batch-draw, not manual draw.rect.

Call .set_hit_box_from_texture() so collision matches the art, not a raw rectangle.

Parallax Background Layers

Create 3 background layers (e.g. school silhouette, playground props, street foreground).

Move each at a different fraction of camera scroll (0.2×, 0.5×, 1×) to give depth.

Frame-Based Animation & Tweening

Animate all characters with real sprite frames (walking frames, idle, hit).

Use arcade.animate() or a tweening helper to smoothly interpolate positions and special-effect scales.

Antialiasing & Smoothing

Render to a high-res offscreen buffer (e.g. 1200×1600), then downscale to 600×800 with bilinear filtering for crisp, anti-aliased edges.

Enable arcade.enable_blending() for proper transparency.

Polished UI with 9-Slice Panels

Use pre-made panel graphics with nine-patch slicing for score bars, dialogue boxes, and quiz overlays.

Render text with a modern bitmap or vector font (e.g. “Nunito” or “Fredoka One”) rather than the default Arial.

Dynamic Lighting & Bloom Accent

Layer a subtle radial light texture at the player’s position (20% opacity) for a “spotlight” effect.

On power-up, draw a white, translucent ellipse that quickly expands/fades (a quick “bloom” effect).

Particle Effects with GPU Sprites

Use arcade.Particle for confetti, sparkles, and smoke. Pre-define textures (star, circle, leaf) and let the engine batch them.

Cap each system at 100 particles and pool instances to avoid GC pauses.

Shader-Like Transition Effects

For level-intro and quiz panels, use a quick “page-turn” or “wipe” tween (Arcade has built-in arcade.load_texture with transform shaders).

On collision, flash a tinted overlay (e.g. semi-transparent red) for 0.1 s.

Consistent Art Direction & Palette

Choose a 16-color palette (to keep textures small) and have every sprite use only those colors.

Export all art at the same pixel grid (e.g. 32×32 or 64×64 texels) for consistent scale and no blurry stretching.
