Scenario Square-Off is a conversational simulation tool where early childhood teachers interact with a realistic, emotionally intelligent AI coach that helps them reflect on real classroom struggles. It’s not a quiz. It’s a coaching dialogue designed to:

Practice emotional intelligence

Unpack bias or shallow thinking

Improve empathy and classroom problem-solving

Shift fixed mindsets into growth-oriented thinking

🗂️ Core Features:
1. Open-Ended Starter Prompt (AI-initiated)
“What’s something you’re finding really difficult in your classroom right now?”
“If you could change one behavior in a child you work with—what would it be?”

AI listens, summarizes the pain point

Validates their feeling with a compassionate tone

Then responds with targeted follow-up questions to unpack context

2. Emotional Reflection Layer
If the user shares frustration (“He hits me and hates me”), the AI responds:

“That sounds painful. Can you tell me more about what happens before he hits you?”
“Do you think there might be a need he’s trying to express?”

If teacher blames or labels (“He’s just bad”), AI challenges gently:

“What makes you feel that way? Has he ever responded differently before?”
“What do you think might be going on behind that behavior?”

3. Adaptive Insight Engine
Depending on their responses, the AI can:

Offer educational insights (“Sometimes hitting can be a form of sensory seeking or a stress release.”)

Ask diagnostic questions (“Has he been sleeping well? Is there a pattern?”)

Suggest strategies (“Would you be open to trying a calm corner or giving him a helper job?”)

4. Positive Reinforcement
If the teacher shows reflective growth, AI praises:

“That’s a powerful insight. It takes a great teacher to think about root causes.”

If the teacher struggles, AI never shames, but guides:

“Let’s explore this together—what would it feel like to try something different next time?”

🧠 Example Interaction:
AI: “What’s one behavior in your classroom that’s been the hardest to manage?”
Teacher: “There’s a kid who hits me. He hates me.”
AI: “Wow, that must be frustrating—and painful too. Why do you think he’s reacting that way?”
Teacher: “I don’t know. Maybe he’s just mean.”
AI: “Has he always acted that way? Do you ever notice times when he’s calmer or more connected?”
Teacher: “Sometimes he’s okay after snack.”
AI: “Interesting. That might tell us something. Do you think hunger or routine could be part of what’s going on?”
